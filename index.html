WORLD MODELS · EMBODIED INTELLIGENCE

Understanding the world.
Learning to act.

My research interests lie at the intersection of world models and embodied intelligence. I study how an agent can maintain a coherent understanding of a changing environment, reason about what it cannot observe, and use predictions to guide action.

I am particularly interested in multi-view scene dynamics, decision-making under partial observability, and adaptation that preserves previously learned knowledge.

01

Research directions

Three connected questions about learning models of the physical world.

SPACE × TIME

Representation & dynamics

A coherent world across views and time

How can multiple observations refer to the same evolving scene? I am interested in persistent scene representations, object identity, and the consistency of multi-view predictions.

A question I care about+

When an object becomes occluded or changes viewpoint, what should a model remember, and which new observations should revise that memory?

BELIEF → ACTION

Uncertainty & decision-making

Planning beyond what is visible

An object that is not observed may still be present. I study how belief over hidden states and alternative futures can support decisions, particularly in autonomous driving and embodied planning.

A question I care about+

Which uncertainties actually change a decision, and when is obtaining another observation more valuable than taking an action immediately?

ADAPT × RETAIN

Continual learning & generalization

Learning what changed, keeping what matters

When the environment changes, an agent needs to adapt without overwriting useful knowledge. I am interested in identifying what changed, deciding when to learn, and transferring knowledge across new conditions.

A question I care about+

What interaction evidence is needed before committing a lasting model update, and how can we evaluate adaptation and retention together?

Conceptual illustrations of research interests; not experimental results.

02

Research perspective

A world model should be judged not just by the futures it predicts, but by the decisions it helps an agent make.

This motivates a connected view of representation, uncertainty, and control: what a model remembers shapes what it predicts; what remains uncertain shapes how an agent should act; and interaction provides evidence for what the model should learn next.

Across these questions, I value explicit assumptions, matched experimental controls, and evaluation that connects model behavior to downstream decisions.
